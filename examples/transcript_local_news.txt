Good evening, and thanks for joining us. I'm at City Hall, where the council voted six to one tonight to approve next year's budget.
The plan sets aside four point two million dollars for road repairs, most of it on the east side, where residents have complained about potholes for years.
The chair of the finance committee said the money comes from a one-time state grant and from delaying the new parking garage by two years.
The one vote against came from a council member who represents the downtown district. He told us he supports the road work, but he worries that pushing back the garage will hurt businesses there.
The budget also adds two positions at the public library and keeps property tax rates unchanged. City staff presented figures showing that tax revenue grew about three percent last year, slightly below what the city projected in the spring.
Several residents spoke during the public comment period. Most of them asked about the timeline for the repairs. The public works director said crews will start in April and should finish by the end of September, weather permitting. A detailed schedule will be posted on the city website next week.
We asked the mayor's office whether the delay could affect the downtown renovation plan. A spokesperson said the city will review that question with business owners at a meeting on the fourteenth.
Looking ahead to the weekend, the farmers market returns on Saturday morning at the fairgrounds, and organizers expect more than forty vendors.
